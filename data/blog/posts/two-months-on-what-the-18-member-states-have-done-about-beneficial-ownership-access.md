On 25 September 2026, the European Commission [sent letters of formal notice](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/inf_26_1834) to 18 member states: Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Czechia, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Lithuania, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania and Spain.

Each country had either missed transposition deadlines for parts of the [Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401640#art_11) that govern who may see beneficial ownership registers or failed to notify the Commission about changes made. The letters gave them two months to reply and complete the job. That window closes on Wednesday 25 November.

This post sets out what each of the 18 has done since 25 September, using the tracker [OpenCheck](https://opencheck.world/) keeps of beneficial ownership access across Europe.

The short answer: Austria has new legitimate-interest rules in force. Greece has passed a law whose access route has not started yet. Germany, Poland and the Netherlands have bills in parliament. Bulgaria closed a consultation, and Estonia announced a change of plan. For the other eleven, I found no new step in the two months.

## What the Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive is

The Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive - [Directive (EU) 2024/1640](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1640/oj/eng) - is one part of the [EU’s 2024 anti-money laundering package](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/financial-crime/anti-money-laundering-and-countering-financing-terrorism-overview_en). The package also includes the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624), which sets the rules that obliged entities such as banks must follow, and the regulation creating the new EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority, AMLA. The directive deals with what member states themselves must run: their financial intelligence units, their supervisors and their central registers, including the beneficial ownership registers.

Those registers have had a turbulent few years. Under the Fifth Anti-Money Laundering Directive, every member state had to make its register of company beneficial owners open to the general public. But in November 2022, the Court of Justice of the European Union struck down that general public access in the [*WM and Sovim* judgment](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex:62020CJ0037). Several registers closed to the public within days.

The 2024 package rebuilt access around a narrower idea: anyone who can [show a legitimate interest](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401640#art_12) may see the register. The directive names [groups](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401640#art_12) who are presumed to have one, including journalists, civil society organisations and academics working on money laundering, and people who may do business with the company concerned. It also sets the procedure: a decision within a fixed number of working days, approval that lasts for a set period, and recognition of another member state’s approval.

## The transposition dates

The directive comes into force in stages. The ones that matter for register access are:

- **10 July 2025**: Article 74, which amends the old directive so that a legitimate-interest route exists in law while the rest is built
- **10 July 2026**: Articles 11, 12, 13 and 15, the detailed rules on access: who gets in, on what grounds, by what procedure, and the exceptions
- **10 July 2027**: most of the rest of the directive, the same day the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation starts to apply

The September letters concern the 10 July 2026 articles. The Commission’s [announcement](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/inf_26_1834) said that “18 Member States have not declared full transposition of the aforementioned articles by the legal deadline”.

Two points are worth keeping in mind when reading what follows:

- First, an infringement for non-communication is about notifying the Commission of complete transposing measures. A country can already run some form of legitimate-interest access - as France and Luxembourg do - and still be named, because its law does not yet match all four articles or it has not notified them
- Second, the next step, if a reply does not satisfy the Commission, is a reasoned opinion and then a possible referral to the Court of Justice. The September letters ended: “In the absence of a satisfactory response, the Commission may decide to issue a reasoned opinion.” What it does after 25 November, and when, is not yet known.

One more official record is worth checking: EUR-Lex [lists](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/NIM/?uri=CELEX:32024L1640) the national measures each country has notified to the Commission for the directive. Since 25 September, four of the 18 have notified something there. Austria notified its amending law (BGBl. I Nr. 94/2026) on 6 November and constitutional provisions on 17 November. Greece notified Law 5354/2026 on 19 November. Spain notified a royal decree on 29 October, against the 2027 deadline, not the access articles. Lithuania notified existing laws and orders on 10 November, under both the 2025 and 2026 deadlines, with no new act among them. Croatia, Estonia and Poland had notified no measures at all as of 23 November.

## Country by country

### **In force**

*Austria* - The amendment to the Beneficial Owners Register Act (WiEReG) was published as BGBl. I Nr. 94/2026 on 29 October 2026. Its new section 10, “Einsicht bei Vorliegen eines berechtigten Interesses” (inspection on a legitimate interest), and the procedure in section 10b came into force on 5 November 2026, and also apply to applications filed before that date. The government’s business service portal says the procedure “erfüllt die Vorgaben der 6. Geldwäscherichtlinie” (meets the requirements of the Sixth Anti-Money Laundering Directive): binding decision periods of 12 working days, decisions valid across the EU, and automated certificates. The finance ministry’s page now says the regime applies “seit 5. November 2026”. The wider Beneficial Owners Register Act 2027 follows mainly from 10 July 2027. Austria is the one country of the 18 that moved from law to working access inside the two months. Sources: [Parliament, I/622](https://www.parlament.gv.at/gegenstand/XXVIII/I/622) · [USP](https://www.usp.gv.at/aktuelles/gesetzliche-neuerungen/Bundesgesetzblatt/wiereg-2027.html) · [BMF](https://www.bmf.gv.at/services/wiereg/berechtigtes-Interesse.html)

## Law passed, access route not yet running

*Greece -* The Ministry of National Economy and Finance tabled an omnibus bill on 26 October 2026. Parliament passed it on 5 November and it was published as Law 5354/2026 (ΦΕΚ Α΄ 184, 9 November 2026). The chapter on the Central Register of Beneficial Owners, described as harmonising with Directive (EU) 2024/1640, moves the register to the Independent Authority for Public Revenue (AADE). Anyone with a specific legitimate interest (“ειδικό έννομο συμφέρον”) can apply to the AADE Governor, and journalists, civil society organisations and academics are named. A decision is due within 12 working days, approval lasts three years, and the company is not told. Until now, access for others needed a prosecutor’s order. The new route has not opened. In the consultation draft, the transfer to AADE takes effect only once a joint ministerial decision confirms the system is ready. Whether the final law keeps that condition, and when the decision will come, I could not confirm [uncertain: ΦΕΚ text not checked]. The details above come from the parliamentary record and press summaries of the final bill text. Source: [Hellenic Parliament, bill page](https://www.hellenicparliament.gr/Nomothetiko-Ergo/Anazitisi-Nomothetikou-Ergou?law_id=21cf1a39-09ed-4b79-9fba-b4f0001d94e6)

## Bills in parliament

*Germany -* Germany puts Articles 11 to 13 and 15 into sections 23 and 23a of the Money Laundering Act (GwG) through the Customs Financial Justice Act (Zollfinanzgerechtigkeitsgesetz, BT-Drs. 21/8282). The Bundestag’s finance committee amended it on 11 November, and the Bundestag passed it on 12 November 2026. The Bundesrat and publication are still to come; it is planned to take effect on 1 January 2027. Separately, on 28 October 2026 the finance ministry published a draft Money Laundering Prevention Act (Geldwäschebekämpfungsgesetz, GwBG), which would replace the GwG on 10 July 2027 and carry the same access rules forward as sections 25 and 26. Its consultation closed on 19 November, and cabinet adoption is planned for 16 December. Germany’s Transparency Register already accepts applications on legitimate interest, under the pre-2024 rules. Sources: [Bundestag](https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2026/kw45-pa-finanzen-zoll-1225840) · [BMF, GwBG draft](https://www.bundesfinanzministerium.de/Content/DE/Gesetzestexte/Gesetze_Gesetzesvorhaben/Abteilungen/Abteilung_VII/21_Legislaturperiode/2026-10-28-E6EUGWRL/1-Referentenentwurf.pdf?__blob=publicationFile&v=3)

*Poland -* Poland restricted access to its Central Register of Beneficial Owners (CRBR) on 1 July 2026, with no route yet for anyone else. The government bill amending the AML/CFT Act adds a new article 68d, giving access to anyone who shows a “uzasadniony interes” (justified interest). The Council of Ministers sent it to the Sejm in September; it became Sejm print no. 3163, dated 28 September and delivered on 5 October. It had its first reading on 9 October and its second on 5 November, and the Sejm passed it on 6 November 2026 (281 for, 21 against, 146 abstaining). It now goes to the Senate and then to publication. The National Revenue Administration says the rules will generally apply from 1 January 2027. Sources: [Sejm, process 3163](https://api.sejm.gov.pl/sejm/term10/processes/3163) · [RCL, UC75](https://legislacja.rcl.gov.pl/projekt/12396501/katalog/13120732)

*Netherlands -* Public access to the Dutch UBO register ended on 16 July 2025, and there is still no route for anyone with a legitimate interest. The decree that would open one (Wijzigingsbesluit toegang UBO-registers, WGK015141) received the Council of State’s advice in July and is still waiting for publication. The new step in the two months came on 20 November 2026: the government sent the Implementation Act on the Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (WGK027204) to the House of Representatives. It carries the other directive provisions on the UBO register: verification, discrepancy handling, and access to historical information for journalists, civil society and academics. It also answers one of the Council of State’s objections, so that the Chamber of Commerce tells a beneficial owner about a consultation only if the owner asks, as Article 12(4) of the directive allows. The ministry expects it to take effect in July 2027. The bill does not itself open legitimate-interest access, which still depends on the decree. Sources: [Wetgevingskalender WGK015141](https://wetgevingskalender.overheid.nl/Regeling/WGK015141) · [WGK027204](https://wetgevingskalender.overheid.nl/Regeling/WGK027204) · [Bill documents](https://open.overheid.nl/details/03d15279-75ac-45f9-a936-c946f8ef66a6)

## Drafts and plans

*Bulgaria -* The State Agency for National Security (ДАНС) put a draft amendment to the Measures Against Money Laundering Act (ЗМИП) out for public consultation, stated to align with Directive (EU) 2024/1640. It sets three levels of access, including persons who can prove a legitimate interest (a new article 63д). The consultation ran from 2 October to 2 November 2026 and is closed. As of early November there was no report on comments and no bill in the National Assembly. The outcome of the consultation is not yet known. Bulgaria already lets people apply on legitimate interest under its existing law. Source: [strategy.bg, consultation 12770](https://www.strategy.bg/bg/public-consultations/12770)

*Estonia -* Estonia is the odd one out: its beneficial ownership data is still open to everyone. A ministerial regulation to restrict it to legitimate-interest access was due on 10 July 2026 and was postponed on the day. On 2 November 2026 ERR reported that the Ministry of Finance still plans the restriction but will now do it by amending the law rather than by regulation, after comments from the Ministries of Justice and Digital Affairs. The ministry would vet applications itself and give priority to journalists. No draft and no date have been published. Until a law passes, the register stays open. Source: [ERR, 2 November 2026](https://www.err.ee/1610183528/rahandusministeerium-plaanib-tegelike-kasusaajate-peitmiseks-muuta-seadust)

## No new step found

For these eleven countries I found no law, bill, draft or consultation on register access dated between 25 September and 23 November 2026. That is a statement about what I could find in official sources, not proof that nothing happened. Several of the sites concerned block automated checks, and a country may have notified the Commission of measures without publishing anything new:

- *Belgium -* People with a legitimate interest have been able to apply to the Treasury since February 2023, under national conditions that predate the directive. I found no transposition of Articles 11 to 13 and 15. A 2025 draft royal decree on trust-like arrangements implements only Article 74.
- *Croatia* *-* Access is limited to authorities and obliged entities. No draft found.
- *Cyprus -* Law 25(I)/2026 of March 2026 put legitimate interest into the law, implementing Article 74 only. No working application route has been confirmed, and nothing new was found.
- *Czechia -* The government discussed an amendment on 22 June 2026 that would give access to people who prove a legitimate interest to the Ministry of Justice. The minister then expected it to take effect in November 2026; officials later said not before the first half of 2027. The draft’s bill had its first reading on 15 October 2026 and discussed by the Constitutional and Legal Guarantee Committee on 30 October 2026. Further discussion may happen after 15 December 2026 according to the bill page.
- *Finland -* Access is limited to authorities and obliged entities; regulations are pending. No government proposal on the beneficial owner register found.
- *France -* INPI has run a legitimate-interest procedure since May 2025. Nothing new found, and the Commission’s letter does not say what France is still missing.
- *Lithuania -* The JADIS register offers access on legitimate interest. No new act found. On 10 November, Lithuania notified the Commission of existing laws and orders as its transposing measures, including the Law on the Prevention of Money Laundering and the Justice Minister’s order on JADIS.
- *Luxembourg -* The law of 23 January 2025 codified legitimate-interest access, with narrower categories than the directive. Nothing new found.
- *Portugal -* Decree-Law 115/2025 moved the register to legitimate-interest access, but the regulations needed to run it have not appeared. Nothing new found.
- *Romania -* Law 86/2025 introduced legitimate-interest access by transposing Article 74. No procedure has been published, and no new draft found.
- *Spain -* Access is restricted with no legitimate-interest route yet. Royal Decree 813/2026 of 7 October (BOE 8 October, in force 28 October) partially transposes the directive, but its changes are to supervision, the financial intelligence unit and statistics, and it does not change who may see the register. It now requires annual statistics on legitimate-interest requests to the register, and Spain notified it against the 2027 deadline. No draft on register access found.

## Around the 18

Two countries outside the list are worth a mention. Ireland, which was not named in September, opened legitimate-interest access to its Register of Beneficial Ownership on 10 November 2026 under [SI 406/2026](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2026/si/406/made/en/print), with a three-year certificate and a 12-working-day decision period. Meanwhile in Italy, the decree transposing the access articles has been in force since July, but the access system is still waiting for the ministerial measure that declares it operational, and a wider transposition decree is out for consultation until 14 December.

## What happens next

The reply period ends on 25 November. After that, the Commission may close a case or issue a reasoned opinion; it has not said when it will decide. Germany and Poland both aim for 1 January 2027. Greece’s start date is still to be confirmed. The Netherlands needs its decree. And for most of the eleven countries with nothing new, the next fixed date is 10 July 2027, when the Anti-Money Laundering Regulation applies and the rest of the directive is due.
