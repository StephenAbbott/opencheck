title: A FollowTheMoney record that names several countries no longer gets the first one as its jurisdiction.
readme: OpenSanctions and OpenAleph records naming several countries no longer read an English bank as Iranian.
tests: backend +14
---
OpenSanctions' and OpenAleph's records of Bank Saderat PLC, an English PLC (GB-COH 01126618), carry `country: ["ir", "gb"]`, and `_ftm_jurisdiction` took the first value: three of the subject's statements said Iran, and FATF_BLACK_LIST / EU_HIGH_RISK_THIRD_COUNTRY listed IR with `position: "subject"` on them (production, 24 Nov 2026). Option 1 on the Notion ticket: the jurisdiction now follows the rule `ftm_registration_country` has followed since 26 Oct — the first property the record fills (`jurisdiction`, then `country`) must name exactly one country (a subdivision and its own country, `US-DE` beside `US`, are one; the subdivision is kept); with several, the record has no jurisdiction and a `commenting` annotation on `/recordDetails` lists the countries the source named, in its order. Nothing is inferred. Shared by every FtM source (OpenSanctions, OpenAleph). Measured on the production lookups of the three anchors named on the ticket: Bank Saderat PLC keeps FATF_BLACK_LIST and EU_HIGH_RISK_THIRD_COUNTRY from Bank Saderat Iran above it and loses only the three false subject entries; Persia International Bank's FATF / EU entries all come from its owners (Companies House and OpenSanctions); Rosneft's EU high-risk and EU tax entries come from GLEIF, MEIP, ClimateTRACE and Wikidata as well, so no curated chip or verdict changes. After deploy, check Bank Saderat PLC's FATF evidence lists IR at `above` only.
