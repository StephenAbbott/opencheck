---
title: The web app serves its own fonts instead of loading them from Google Fonts.
readme: The web app now serves Bitter, DM Sans and DM Mono itself, so a first visit no longer waits on Google Fonts.
---
Step 3 of the "Speed up first load of the opencheck.world homepage" ticket. `index.html` loaded a render-blocking stylesheet from fonts.googleapis.com and its woff2 files from fonts.gstatic.com — two more origins (DNS + TLS each) on a cold visit, neither cached at the edge. `frontend/src/fonts.css` (imported first in `main.tsx`) now declares the three families from woff2 files copied from Fontsource 5.3.0 into `frontend/src/assets/fonts/`: Bitter and DM Sans as the variable fonts Google already served, DM Mono at the 400 and 500 weights the old link asked for, every subset Google served (Bitter's Cyrillic, Cyrillic-ext and Vietnamese included) with `unicode-range`, and `font-display: swap`. The family names are the ones Google served — "Bitter", "DM Sans", "DM Mono" — because Tailwind, `index.css` and the Cytoscape stylesheet in `BODSGraph.tsx` all ask for them by name. Vite hashes the files into `/assets/`, so they get the immutable year-long cache and the edge cache. They are not preloaded, on purpose: with swap the text paints at once in the fallback face, and a preload would compete with the entry chunk for bandwidth on a slow connection. Checked in a production build at 1280 and 390 px: the homepage downloads three font files (the Latin subsets), all same-origin, h1 in Bitter and body in DM Sans, and makes no request to Google. The OFL texts sit beside the files and `ATTRIBUTIONS.md` names them; `index.html`'s comment called the fonts CC BY 4.0, which they are not. Not changed: the server-rendered entity pages (`entity_pages.py`) and the feature-image script still load Google Fonts; the entity pages are a follow-up on the ticket.
