Part C of the "Watchlist as pKYC: materiality, context and review record" ticket (Notion) — Moody's recasting of periodic review as confirmation that monitoring caught what it should. The watchlist never polls, so "nothing happened" to a watched company could mean nothing changed or that no delta named it, and its one time-based rule ran the other way: a list nobody opened for 90 days was deleted silently — exactly the lists whose owners most needed a nudge. New module `opencheck/watchlist_review.py`: `review_of` reads what the store already holds — `last_checked_at` as the clock (the last full re-run, when every source was asked), the snapshot's `checked[]` liveness and `retrieved_at` per source, its `degraded_sources` — and words one line per watch, computed on read and never stored: "Last fully re-run 112 days ago; 2 of 3 sources answered from an older copy (UK Companies House 3 Jun 2026 and …); KvK not reached. Re-check by hand to refresh." A watch never re-run says "Baseline taken N days ago and never re-run — no delta has named this company since." The line never says the company is unchanged. Stephen's decisions (17 Oct 2026): one instance-wide `OPENCHECK_WATCHLIST_REVIEW_DAYS` (90), never tied to findings; the clock is the last full re-run; due-ness is a state, so no entry per due watch — the count rides in the feed `<subtitle>` and on the page; the stale rule becomes notice-then-delete — at `OPENCHECK_WATCHLIST_STALE_DAYS` (90) unopened the worker writes one entry per list in a new `review` tier ("A note about this list, not about a company", materiality note, empty LEI, headline "This list: nobody has opened it for a while — it will be deleted unless opened") and stamps `lists.expiry_notice_at` (migration 5); `OPENCHECK_WATCHLIST_DELETE_GRACE_DAYS` (30) later it deletes; any token-bearing request withdraws the notice (`touch` clears it) and the entry stays as history; an empty list gets no notice; the same rule for every list; the 404 reads "A list that nobody opens or polls for 90 days is warned in its feed, and deleted 30 days later." A feed reader that polls keeps its list alive indefinitely, so the rule reaches only the owner who comes back late — which is why the notice has a grace period rather than a single longer window. Shown as `review` on every watch and a list-level `review` block on `/watch/{token}`, the sentence under each row with a warn-tone "Review due" chip and a list-level line on `/watchlist`, the count in the Atom subtitle, and `watches_review_due` / `lists_expiring` / `lists_deleted_total` on `/watchstats`. `docs/watchlist.md` gains "Periodic review: when OpenCheck last looked" and the stale-list paragraph is rewritten; `REVIEW_WORDS` and the tier vocabulary are mirrored in `lib/watchlist.ts` and pinned by `test_watchlist_review.py`. No automatic re-run (`/recheck` is the action), no "mark reviewed" (stage D), no e-mail.
