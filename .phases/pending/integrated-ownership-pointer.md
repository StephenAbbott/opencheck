title: The integrated-ownership annotation targets /recordDetails and goes on OpenCheck's own GLEIF statement.
readme: The integrated-ownership note now points at a statement's record details, so libcove no longer rejects it, and is written only on OpenCheck's own GLEIF statement, never on Open Ownership's.
tests: backend +4
---
Found by the BODS quality sweep of 19 Oct 2026, the first since the integrated-ownership band shipped: eleven `bods_additional` warnings (`gleif` ×9, `GLEIF` ×2), all libcove's `annotation_statement_pointer_target_invalid` with pointer `/` — on ASDA Stores, BP, Rosneft, Birtley Investment, Eesti Energia and the other lookup subjects with an integrated figure. `integrated_ownership.apply_to_bods` wrote `statementPointerTarget: "/"`, and the Phase 339 test pinned it. A statement-wide annotation targets `/recordDetails` (the Phase 255 rule); the test now asserts that, and a new test fails on `/` and on any pointer that does not resolve inside the statement. No other annotation in the backend targets `/`. Saved reports keep the annotation they were folded with; a new run, or a replay after the cache window, carries the fixed pointer. Where it goes (Stephen, 20 Oct 2026, option c): `annotation_target` picks OpenCheck's own GLEIF entity statement for the subject — stamped `opencheckSourceId: "gleif"` and published by OpenCheck — and, on a register-anchored run with no GLEIF anchor, the first subject statement OpenCheck published. It never writes on another publisher's statement: on BP and Rosneft it had been written onto a copy of Open Ownership's verbatim GLEIF statement, against Phase 317's rule. The stamped id is read, not the description, because Open Ownership's GLEIF statements are described "GLEIF" too; with no OpenCheck subject statement there is no annotation. The PermID `publicListing` (Phase 236) still picks by `source_id_of` and was not changed. Check the next weekly BODS quality sweep reports no `annotation_statement_pointer_target_invalid`.
