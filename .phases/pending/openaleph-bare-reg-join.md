---
title: A bare FtM registration number now joins the subject in a country the record itself lists.
readme: OpenAleph and OpenSanctions records that file a registration number with several countries now join the company they describe in any of those countries.
tests: backend +4, vitest +1
---
Bank Saderat PLC's OFAC SDN and US Trade CSL records in OpenAleph file `01126618` with `country: [ir, gb]`, so `ftm_registration_country` leaves the number as a bare `REG`, and a bare `REG` gave no `JUR:` key. The two records joined the subject's referent group only when OpenAleph's own Companies House record came back with them; a run without it named two subjects (the BODS quality sweep, `declaration_subject_not_subject|openaleph`, every week since 2 Nov). PR 697 already stamps such a record as its source's subject when the lookup accepted it on a shared identifier; this is the join itself, option 1 on the ticket. `reconcile._identifier_keys` and the frontend `identKeys` now give a bare `REG` on an OpenAleph or OpenSanctions statement a `JUR:<c>:<n>` key for each country the statement names (its jurisdiction and its addresses' countries), never for any other. Companies House's bare `REG` (a corporate officer whose country is read from a service address) is left out. Saderat's sanctions-list records are therefore in the subject's group on every run, and on the canvas they merge with the company instead of drawing a second Bank Saderat. Their IR jurisdiction is the separate FtM-jurisdiction ticket; `companies_house|openaleph` and `gleif|openaleph` are not shown jurisdiction pairs, so no disagreement is displayed meanwhile.
