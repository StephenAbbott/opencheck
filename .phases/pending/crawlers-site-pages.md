From a Search Console export of 24 Nov 2026: indexed pages fell from about 123,000 in early September to 337, nearly all of them entity pages Google crawled and chose not to keep ("Crawled – currently not indexed" 129,938; "Discovered – currently not indexed" 936,781). Nothing blocks search or AI crawlers: robots.txt is one `User-agent: *` group, and the Phase 144 bot gate covers only the four interactive streams. Stephen's decision: keep the gate and the lookup limits, fix the three cheap gaps here, and treat entity-page quality as its own ticket. (1) robots.txt no longer disallows `/share`: LinkedInBot and Twitterbot read robots.txt before fetching a preview card, so every shared check went without one. The share pages already carry `noindex`, so they stay out of search results; a test pins both. (2) The SPA served every client-routed page as the homepage (title "OpenCheck", canonical `/`), so Google folded /features, /about, /api, /sources and /changelog into `/`. `opencheck/site_pages.py` holds the pages (title, description), `render_shell` rewrites the shell's title, description, canonical, Open Graph and Twitter tags for a known path in `spa_fallback` and the `/sources` view negotiation, and the titles are pinned to `VIEW_DOCUMENT_TITLES` in `lib/views.ts` by parsing it. The homepage and report URLs keep the shell as built. (3) `/sitemap.xml` (now generated, replacing the static two-URL file, and named in robots.txt) lists the homepage, the seven pages and the blog; `/llms.txt` describes OpenCheck, its pages, the MCP server and the API, and asks agents to use the API rather than crawl report pages. Both routes sit above the IndexNow `/{key}.txt` route, which would otherwise answer /llms.txt. After deploy: submit `/sitemap.xml` in Search Console and request indexing for /features and /about; check a shared link in LinkedIn's Post Inspector.
