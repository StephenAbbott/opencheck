Two more of RIK's daily open-data files go into the Estonian index: the registration department's rulings (`maarused`, 2.9 million, 1.8 GB unzipped) and the commercial pledges (`kommertspandid`). Stephen's decisions (16 Oct 2026): a finding sentence and BODS notes, never a signal; the ticket's types — annual-report deletion and fine warnings, annual-report fines and repeat fines, the deletion warning published in Ametlikud Teadaanded, the annual-report deficiency ruling, the net-assets dissolution warning — plus X1 and every other compulsory-dissolution warning, always in English words with the register's term, never a code. Only rulings in force (`J`) are kept: `L`, "deficiencies remedied", is the answered majority (95,760 of 106,757 annual-report deletion warnings). The register keeps a paid fine "in force", so an annual-report ruling is set aside once the company has filed a report for a year ending within 18 months before it; dissolution warnings stand. Measured on the 16 Oct 2026 files: 148,469 rulings kept on 40,684 companies, 100,163 shown on 19,162. The latest ruling, short and dated, replaces the "in the register since" lead ("Annual-report deletion warning 12 March 2026; …"), and follows the counts under a liquidation lead or when it would push them past the 140-character cap; a note on the company lists every shown ruling with its number. Each current pledge (8,068 on 4,945 companies; EEK on pre-2011 pledges, as filed) is a note — amount, rank, date, pledgee by name and registry code — and the finding counts them where there is room; a pledgee is never a relationship or a party, and the 35 natural-person pledgees, filed with a personal code, are stored as a kind only and worded "a private individual". Building the full index for this found a download cut short (73,269,248 of 74,262,843 bytes) that the zip magic-number check let through: a download now checks its Content-Length and the zip directory and is retried. The index grows by 18 MB (1.8 MB gzipped, 151 MB in all); an index built before the tables answers `None`.
