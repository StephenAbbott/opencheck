Found by the MCP testing of 7 Nov 2026 (Notion "MCP testing ahead of Connected Data London 2026", issue 2): Taqa Bratani Limited's `opencheck_owners` said "No party is calculated at or above the threshold. 0 current declared beneficial owner(s)" while Companies House names The Abu Dhabi Government as its person with significant control and the same lookup's `disclosure_status` said "Beneficial owners: Declared (1)". The PSC is a relevant legal entity: its interest carries `beneficialOwnershipOrControl: false` by the regime's own definition (`bo_regimes` `psc_corporate_rle`), so the beneficial-owner list rightly leaves it out — and nothing else listed it. A fourth list, `registered_controllers` (Stephen's choice over folding entities into the beneficial-owner list), carries every relationship to the subject from a `bo_register` source whose interested party is an entity statement and whose interest says `beneficialOwnershipOrControl: false` and is not an officer role — today Companies House RLEs, Nigeria CAC corporate PSCs and DC beneficial-owner entities. Consolidation (GLEIF), crowdsourced (Wikidata) and securities sources are never in it, nor is a person flagged false (Estonia's J/C fallback board members). Each row: name, LEI where the statement carries one, jurisdiction, source, interest words, current / ended. `in_both` now reads both register lists. The summary names the registered controllers ("Named on a beneficial-ownership register as a controlling legal entity, not a natural person: …") and the current declared beneficial owners, and when every list is empty it says what the lookup's `disclosure_status` declares per source ("What the sources say about owners: SEC EDGAR — Shareholders: Declared (3).") so the answer never reads as "no owners" beside a source that has them. `owners_answer` takes `disclosure=` (the MCP tool passes the lookup's block). The tool docstring, the /api page summary and the generated data dictionary say four lists.
