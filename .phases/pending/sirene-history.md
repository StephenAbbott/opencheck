---
title: France's Sirene history periods reach the History tab.
readme: The History tab now reads INSEE's Sirene history periods, so French renames, legal-form changes, cessations and head-office moves appear for every French SIREN.
tests: backend +14, vitest +3
---
The `sirene` adapter already received every period of a legal unit from `/siren/{siren}` and threw all but the current one away; it now keeps them (an allowlist with no person's name fields, cache `sirene/unit-v2`) and `timeline/sirene.py` compares each period with the one before, so the History tab costs no extra INSEE request. Measured on INSEE's StockUniteLegaleHistorique of 1 Nov 2026 over 5,998 French LEIs: only a change from one real value to another is an event, because INSEE's change flags mostly mark a value recorded for the first time (204 of 249 state changes) or fire with no change at all (1,532 of 1,809 société-à-mission flags). Stephen's decisions (11 Nov 2026): legal-form changes are notable (SA → SAS 388 in the sample); head-office moves are administrative rows naming the new SIRET, without the address; changes INSEE dated by its yearly processing (1 January up to 2008, or 25 December — 900 of 2,198 renames) read "recorded by INSEE in <year>", never a day; activity changes are administrative only within one NAF version, so the NAF 2025 switch on 1 Jan 2027 needs no special case; trading name, ESS and société à mission are administrative and only on N ↔ O; a sole trader's name is never shown. The History service derives the SIREN from RA000189, RA000192 and RA001129 and links rows to the Annuaire des entreprises. Entity clustering no longer folds two changes from one register with different outcomes into one row: TOTAL SA → TOTAL SE (29 May 2020) and TOTAL SE → TOTALENERGIES SE (28 May 2021) are 364 days apart and used to merge; GLEIF's rename now joins the row with the same outcome. After deploy, open TotalEnergies (529900S21EQ1BO4ESM68) on History: four Sirene renames, the SA → SE form change, and GLEIF corroborating the 2021 rename.
