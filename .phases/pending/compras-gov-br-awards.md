`sources/compras_gov_br.py`, `scripts/build_compras_gov_br_index.py`, `.github/workflows/refresh-compras-gov-br-index.yml` (weekly, Wednesdays, release `compras-gov-br-index`), `bods/mappers/brazil.py::map_compras_gov_br`, `finding_compras_gov_br`, `_bh_compras_gov_br` and `ComprasGovBrAwardsList` on the QuickCheck card. Measured on the registry's full file (publication 157, CC BY 4.0, retrieved 27 Oct 2026): 920,868 processes and 1,672,831 awards from August 2021 to September 2026; 95.7% of supplier entries carry a valid CNPJ, 3.9% a CPF. The index holds 172,139 CNPJ roots, 135 MB (46 MB gzipped), built in four and a half minutes. Stephen's decisions A–G (12 Nov 2026, on the Notion ticket): the join is the CNPJ root, so a branch's awards count for the company (Banco do Brasil: 965 awards across 24 establishments against 845 on the head office's number); any Brazilian registration authority filing a CNPJ that passes both check digits (the Receita Federal, the Juntas Comerciais, the CVM, the Central Bank); every supplier root indexed; no supplier name, address or contact point stored, CPF suppliers dropped at build time and CPF-shaped numbers removed from award text; published award values summed as OpenCheck's sum and said as not money paid (the publication has no contract stage and no award status), zero-value awards counted; the buyer shown with its sphere of government (federal, state or municipal); the Compras.gov.br portal as the one link, since the process page's address could not be confirmed. Awards are activity, never ownership: no BODS relationship, no signal, no verdict clause — one entity statement, named from GLEIF, with a `BR-CNPJ` identifier only when Compras.gov.br itself names the establishment GLEIF files. A `BR-CNPJ` register-anchored lookup asks it by the number too. After merge, run the refresh workflow once by hand: until the asset exists `covers_lei` is False and the source is never announced.
