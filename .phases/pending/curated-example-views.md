Each curated card keeps its name, description and finding chips and gains a tile for every view of the report, using the tab's own icon, name and accent colour (`MODE_TABS`) plus a short line on what the view answers (`EXAMPLE_VIEW_ANSWERS` in `lib/exampleCards.ts`). Each tile is a real link to the report's shareable URL (`exampleViewHref`: `/?lei=…`, plus `&mode=…` for anything but QuickCheck), so it opens in a new tab or copies; a plain click stays in the app and runs the lookup straight into that view, and `lookupLei` now writes the requested mode into the address so a refresh lands in the same view. To give the tiles room the homepage shows four examples, not six (Stephen chose option C of three mock-ups): Equinor ASA and Eli Lilly and Company came off the cards and stay in the weekly findings regression as anchors, as BP did. The cards stack one per row on a phone and sit two across from `md` up; a tile stacks its icon above its name, because side by side "BackgroundCheck" overflowed a third of a card at the page's 1100px width. After deploy, check the four cards and a tile click on a phone.
