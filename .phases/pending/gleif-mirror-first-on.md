The second half of the "GLEIF rate limiter refusing a third of stream/export calls" ticket. On 5 Oct 2026 about 31% of the anchor calls the stream and export routes tried were refused by OpenCheck's own 50-a-minute GLEIF budget, and every refusal already sent the anchor to the Golden Copy mirror — so mirror-first changes what a refused lookup reads very little, and what an ordinary one spends a great deal: one live call (the Level 1 record, for the mapping-file ids) instead of six. Two things the mirror path shared with the fallback had to be right first. The store-served bundle read `store.children(lei, limit=100)` while the live path pages `/direct-children` up to ten pages of 100 (Phase 255), so a group of more than 100 direct children lost the rest — Shell's 105 read as 100; it now reads to the same cap, with the store's total. And a cross-reference top-up the mirror could not make (no throttle headroom, a timeout, an upstream error) now names its reason on the bundle, which the previous branch's pipeline check turns into a `gleif` `source_read` degradation saying OpenCorporates was not consulted; a top-up skipped because live mode is off names none. `render.yaml` sets `OPENCHECK_GLEIF_MIRROR_FIRST` and `OPENCHECK_GLEIF_LIVE_CONFIRM` to `true` (the second is measurement only and costs no extra call); the config defaults stay `false`, so tests and local runs are live-first. To check in production: `/mirror` `anchor.mirror` climbing with `anchor.miss_live` small, `anchor.topup_skipped` near zero and `confirm.differs` small with its `differing_paths`; `/signalstats` `gleif.by_route.stream` sending about one call per fresh lookup with `refused_rate_limited` near zero; and a week without a deploy for the ticket's "done when" reading. Going back to live-first is the one `render.yaml` value.
