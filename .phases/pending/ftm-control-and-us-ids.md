FollowTheMoney 4.11.0 added a `Control` edge (controller → controlled, with a free-text `controlType` and a `legalBasis`, and no percentage) and 4.11.3 moved `cikCode` to LegalEntity, so a person can carry one, and `irsCode` (a US EIN) to Organization. OpenCheck's FtM mapper did not know any of them: a Control edge failed the vendored edge-table drift check against the installed model, and CIKs and EINs on OpenSanctions records were dropped. `_FTM_EDGE_SCHEMAS` gains `Control` with a `control` policy: the interest type is the edge's `role` when it is a BODS interest type or a known role word, else its `controlType` (`voting` → votingRights, `appointment` → appointmentOfBoard, `contractual` → rightsGrantedByContract, `nominee` → nominee, `state` → controlByLegalFramework), else otherInfluenceOrControl. `controlType: trust` alone does not say which trust role, so it stays otherInfluenceOrControl; `indirect` sets `directOrIndirect`; the role and each legal basis go to `details`; `beneficialOwnershipOrControl` is never set from FtM. `cikCode` → `US-SEC-CIK` and `irsCode` → `US-EIN` on entity statements. The bods-ftm pin moves to the commit that aligns that package with FtM 4.11.3 (the ticket "BODS FTM updates to align with FollowTheMoney releases", bods-ftm PR #3), which brings followthemoney 4.11.3 and rigour 2.6.2. After deploy, check an OpenSanctions record carrying `cikCode` shows the identifier, and that matching results for the curated examples are unchanged.
