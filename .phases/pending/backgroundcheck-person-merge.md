---
title: BackgroundCheck merges people on identifiers first, and never on a name alone across sources.
readme: BackgroundCheck now merges people on a shared identifier first and on name plus birth year second, and never merges two Wikidata items or two Estonian register person keys into one card.
tests: vitest +13, vitest_files +1
---
From the "BackgroundCheck person-source data quality" ticket (section 2, Wikidata bare-name records), and the precondition Stephen set on 20 Oct 2026 for any new BackgroundCheck person source (Phase F). `extractConnectedPeople` keyed every person on order-insensitive name + birth year, ignoring identifiers, so two same-name records with no birth year always merged: Unilever's Wikidata "Fernando Fernández" card held a Mexican actor's item and another person's, and since the Estonia work two Estonians with the same name, which the register keeps apart by `isikukood_hash`, would have shared a card (the open data carries no birth dates). The new `groupPersonRecords` decides which statements are one person: records sharing any identifier (read from `identifiers` and the Phase 255 `identifying` annotations) are one; a same-name pair merges when both carry a birth year and the years agree, or when neither does and both come from the same source (one register naming one person on several lists, which kept NZ and the Estonian page path merging as before); a name alone never merges across sources, and those pairs stay separate cards that clustering offers at Medium. Nothing merges across two values of a scheme that names one person per value (`ONE_PERSON_PER_VALUE_SCHEMES`: `EE-ARIREGISTER-HASH`, `WIKIDATA`), and `clusterPeople.scorePair` no longer offers such a pair for review. Companies House officer ids are deliberately not in that set: one person can hold several. A birth year on one side and none on the other still does not merge, as before. A card's key stays `name|year` and gains the first statementId only when two people now share it. Measured on production exports of 20 Oct 2026 against the previous rule: Unilever 12 → 13 people (the two Wikidata items split, each still a Medium candidate against the real CFO, never against each other); HSBC unchanged (19 people, no clusters); Tallink unchanged at 14 people, and its two Medium clusters, the Hanschmidt family (four people) and Enn and Eve Pant, are gone because the register gives each a different person key. `frontend/scripts/cluster-eval.mjs` now imports the real modules (run with `npx vite-node`) instead of a copy that had already fallen behind. `docs/background-check.md` states the rule. Section 1 of the ticket (Estonian coverage) was closed by the Estonia plan without code: Tallink now shows 14 people where it showed 4. Option 3 on the ticket (requiring more than a bare name for a Medium cluster against a Wikidata name-only record) was not taken.
