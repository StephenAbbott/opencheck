---
title: KvK open data is paced at one request a minute, instead of being retried into the ground.
readme: KvK open-data requests are now paced at the register's one-request-a-minute quota through a general per-host throttle, so Dutch company cards stop showing placeholder KvK data whenever the minute was already spent.
tests: backend +11
---
The weekly source-health check of 27 Oct 2026 found `kvk` degraded on both of the previous day's sweeps, including the first of the day, and three fresh production lookups that morning showed the gap was not CI-only: ASML Holding and Heineken rendered placeholder KvK data (`degraded_sources: rate_limited`) and ING Groep did not. The register was healthy and answered in 0.64 s from elsewhere. The defect was OpenCheck's pacing. `opendata.kvk.nl` allows **one request per minute per IP** (plus 200 per five minutes across all callers) and answers a spent minute with a bare 429, with no `Retry-After` or `RateLimit-*` headers. Nothing paced it: `build_client()` throttled `api.gleif.org` alone. The adapter's own retry loop waited 2 + 4 + 8 ≈ 14 s against a 60 s window, so once the minute was spent every retry was certain to fail, and each retry was another refused request.

**A general per-host throttle.** New module `opencheck/host_throttle.py`, installed in `build_client()` inside the GLEIF wrapper, so every adapter is covered with no opt-in. It gives each governed host a sliding-window budget, a penalty box fed by observed 429s (the server's `Retry-After` when one is sent, otherwise one full window) and a per-request deadline. A request that cannot get a slot within the wait raises `HostRateLimitedError` **without being sent**, because a request that is known to be refused only extends the refusal. After a 429 the request is re-sent once the penalty has passed, as often as its deadline allows. GLEIF keeps its own throttle (lookup reserve, discretionary calls and statistics are GLEIF-only); a new host joins with one entry in `_LIMIT_READERS` and two settings. KvK's settings are `OPENCHECK_KVK_RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE` (default 1; 0 disables, as the test suite does) and `OPENCHECK_KVK_THROTTLE_MAX_WAIT_S`. The wait defaults to 20 s, under the lookup's 30 s per-source budget: an interactive lookup queues if the next slot is close and otherwise degrades at once.

**The adapter.** `kvk.py` drops its retry loop (`_MAX_RETRIES` and the backoff constants are gone; `request_with_retry` stays for SEC EDGAR). A throttle refusal is recorded as `rate_limited` with its own wording: the minute's request was already used, so nothing was sent. Nothing is cached, under the Phase 262 rule. Concurrent fetches of the **same** KvK number now take a per-number lock and re-read the cache inside it. A lookup and the `/deepen` behind it used to send the same request twice inside one minute, which guaranteed a 429 on the second; now one request is sent and the second reader is served from the cache it fills, through its own provenance scope.

**The sweep.** `source-health.yml` sets `OPENCHECK_KVK_THROTTLE_MAX_WAIT_S=130`, so a batch job queues through up to two refused minutes (a GitHub runner's IP is shared, and its minute may be spent before the probe starts), on the same principle as `OPENCHECK_GLEIF_THROTTLE_MAX_WAIT_S=120`. `SourceProbe` gains `timeout_s`; the sweep uses the larger of it and `--timeout`. The `kvk` probe sets 150 s so `asyncio.wait_for` does not cut the wait short.

**Decided:** KvK's paid authenticated API (€6.40/month plus €0.02 per profile, with higher limits) is not a fallback. It needs a subscriber registered in the Dutch Handelsregister and its terms are not an open licence. OpenCheck has no Dutch-registered entity to sign it, so pacing is the whole fix.

**What still degrades, honestly:** production runs one uvicorn worker, so one process-wide budget is the egress IP's budget. Two **different** Dutch companies looked up inside the same minute still cost the second one its KvK record (shown as `rate_limited`, with a retry button), and so does a FullCheck that reaches several Dutch entities. That is the quota, not a defect; a successful answer is cached, so it is a first-look cost only. **To check in production:** look up ASML Holding (`724500Y6DUVHQD6OXN27`) and, more than a minute later, Heineken N.V. (`724500K5PTPSST86UQ23`): both KvK cards should be live. Then look up a third Dutch company within 40 s of the second: it should either queue and answer, or degrade with the "not sent" wording, never the old "did not answer (HTTP 429)". The next Monday sweep's `kvk` row should be `ok`.
