From the Open Ownership usable-data check (opportunity 2; OO feature 20, "includes non-compliant and exempt companies"): absence from a dataset is not an exemption, and OpenCheck knew the difference through six separate channels — GLEIF reporting exceptions, Companies House PSC statements mapped to unspecifiedReason, Ukraine's filed absences, Estonia's "due, none published", degraded_sources and the knowability access status. `opencheck/disclosure.py` reads them all and emits one `disclosure_status` event per lookup (both pipelines), folded as recorded into `LookupResponse.disclosure_status`, so a saved report replays it. Nine values — the ticket's seven plus `none_filed` (record held, no owner, no reason) and `no_record` (Stephen, 3 Nov 2026) — each entry saying what it covers: beneficial owners, shareholders, or GLEIF's consolidating parent (so GLEIF's exceptions read "Parent: Exempt", never a beneficial-ownership exemption). Stephen's decisions: it describes and raises nothing (OPAQUE_OWNERSHIP and GLEIF_REPORTING_EXCEPTION unchanged; an AST test keeps risk, verdict, signalstats and narrative from importing it); every value a source supports is listed, with no precedence rule (Companies House can be "Declared (2)" and "Not provided" at once); chips on QuickCheck source cards only, with the source's own words beneath, and the sources that are not ownership sources named on one line; `restricted` only for a national register in the subject's country whose beneficial-ownership tier is closed under knowability; BODS gets one commenting annotation on OpenCheck's own subject statement and no new statement. Every ownership source (`DISCLOSES`) is now always deepened. Also in the MCP lookup (`disclosure_status.by_source`), the PDF/Markdown report ("What each source says about owners"), and the findings regression (class `disclosure`; Bank Saderat PLC, Eli Lilly and Shell carry measured floors). Mapping table: `docs/disclosure-status.md`. To check in production: Shell's Companies House card reads "Beneficial owners: None on record" — a company on a UK regulated market keeps no PSC register, and whether OpenCheck should infer that exemption from the PermID listing is a follow-up question.
