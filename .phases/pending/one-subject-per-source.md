Follow-up to Phases 396–398 (the "One source, several records of the subject" ticket; Stephen, 24 Oct 2026: option 1, fold rather than relax the sweep). The post-deploy check of Phase 398 on Ørsted found every source naming one `declarationSubject` except OpenAleph, which named three: three PSC-derived records of Ørsted A/S, each its own hit bundle, each carrying `REG-DK 36213728` and no LEI. The per-bundle stamp rightly gave each its own subject; `stamp_merged` is meant to restamp a source's statements to that source's member of the subject's referent group, but the three never joined the group — `reconcile._identifier_keys` gave a `REG-DK` identifier only its scheme-scoped key, while GLEIF's `DK-CVR 36213728` carried the jurisdiction key `JUR:DK:36213728`, and the two never met. The weekly BODS quality sweep would have failed `declaration_subject_not_subject` on a curated example. The fix is the key, on both sides (`reconcile.py`, `reconcile.ts`): `REG-<country>` is the mappers' fallback for a register number whose register is not named (OpenAleph's `registrationNumber`, a PSC filed as registered in "England And Wales") — a national registration number in that country — so it now bridges to `JUR:<country>:<value>` exactly as a named register scheme does, provided the statement's own jurisdiction, where it has one, is that country; `subject_keys` had read the two spellings as one since Phase 290, so the referent group now agrees with the register-anchored lookup. On the production Ørsted bundle the three OpenAleph records join GLEIF's group, `stamp_merged` names one of them on every OpenAleph statement, and `check_declaration_subjects` is clean; on the FullCheck canvas the four Ørsted nodes become one. No behaviour change elsewhere: the key is added, never removed, and a `REG-DK` number on a statement registered in another country gets no Danish key.
