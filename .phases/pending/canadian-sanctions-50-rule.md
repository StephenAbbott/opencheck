The Canadian sanctions 50% rule ticket (Notion, 12 Nov 2026). Global Affairs Canada's deemed-ownership guidance (August 2026) says listed persons' positions are added together under SEMA s.2.1 and JVCFOA s.2.01: a listed person controls an entity it holds 50% or more of the shares or 50% or more of the voting rights in, directly or indirectly, or whose board it can change, and everything a controlled entity holds is deemed the listed person's. Canada is a fourth row in `opencheck/sanctions_ownership.py`: 50% or more, aggregated, a caught company counting in full, as for OFAC. Stephen's decisions: only `ca_dfatd_sema_sanctions` and `ca_sema_sanctions_news` count (never FACFOA, the Criminal Code terrorist list or UN listings); voting rights are a second limb for Canada, summed on their own and never added to shares; the board limb is read from `appointmentOfBoard` as context only, so it can make the result "may meet" and never "meets"; the EU and UK stay shares-only; one signal with Canada as a fourth row, no new code; `SIGNAL_RULES` 7. A row that rests on votes says "of the voting rights" and carries `limb`; the not-computed note adds Canada's direction limb, holdings passed to associates after listing (GAC's example 4), GAC permits, other Canadian instruments and the foreign-state carve-out. GAC's examples 1–3 are tests. Measured on OpenSanctions' sanctions collection (12 Nov 2026): 1,072 parties are on Canada's lists and none of the OFAC/EU/UK lists read here, but none is an owner on an ownership edge there, so there is no Canada-only golden case; Bank Mellat is on Canada's list too, so the existing anchor, Persia International Bank PLC, should now meet all four regimes. After deploy, check that Persia International Bank's table shows a fourth row, Canada, meeting the test on Bank Mellat's Companies House band, and that the weekly findings regression labels any movement as a rule change.
