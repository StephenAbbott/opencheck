New source `dart_korea` (the Open DART ticket, 10 Nov 2026; Stephen's decisions A–E on the Notion ticket). GLEIF files a Korean company's National Tax Service business registration number (`RA000657`, 10 digits) or its court-registry corporate registration number (`RA000419`, 13 digits); both are now org-id schemes (`KR-BRN`, `KR-CRN`) in `_GLEIF_RA_TO_ORG_ID`, hyphenated as GLEIF and DART write them. Open DART is keyed on its own eight-digit `corp_code`, and its bulk filer list (`corpCode.xml`) carries no registration number, so the join is a weekly crosswalk (`scripts/build_dart_korea_index.py`, `refresh-dart-korea-index.yml`, release `dart-korea-index`, downloaded at boot): a name match against the filer list, accepted only when `company.json` returns the very number GLEIF files (decision A: LEI crosswalk only; the whole-register index is its own ticket). A lookup reads five things live with the key (`OPENDART_API_KEY`, scrubbed as `crtfc_key`): the profile, the periodic-report list, the 5% reports, and from the latest annual, half-year or quarterly report the largest shareholder table (최대주주 및 특수관계인) and the executives table (임원 현황) (decision B). Open DART answers HTTP 200 with its status in the body: `013` is an answer (no data), `020` the key's daily cap (rate_limited), `010`/`011`/`012`/`901` a refused key (not_configured), anything else upstream_error — a degradation, never cached; a complete read is cached a day. OpenCheck spends at most `OPENDART_DAILY_BUDGET` (15,000 of the key's 20,000 a day, counted per Korean day) so the weekly build always has room. Mapping: one shareholding per holder and share class at the period-end percentage, the insurer's special account said in details and never added; executives with name, position, registered or not, full time, tenure end and birth year-month as a partial `birthDate`, sex and career history dropped (decision D); registered directors `boardMember` (`boardChair` for the board chair), the representative director and unregistered executives `seniorManagingOfficial`, auditors officer-marked; a person on both tables is one node, joined by name within the one filing and annotated. A 5% report is the reporter's holding together with its specially related persons, so the figure goes in details and never in `share`; a report below 5% ends the holding. Korea has no public beneficial-ownership register: `beneficialOwnershipOrControl` is never set and nothing calls a holder an owner. Disclosure status reads it as a shareholder list. The knowability row for KR (decision E, verified by Stephen 10 Nov 2026) is synced into `jurisdictions.json` from that one Notion row. A number the crosswalk lacks is a note card; with no crosswalk or no key the source is not announced.
