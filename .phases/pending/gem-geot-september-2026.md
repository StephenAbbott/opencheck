title: GEM's September 2026 release, and two dates that could not be true.
readme: The GEOT artifact is rebuilt from GEM's September 2026 release, a release date that postdates its own file is now refused, and multi-URL status cells are split in both carriers.
tests: backend +17
---
Global Energy Monitor published a September 2026 Global Energy Ownership Tracker while the committed artifact was still the August release, and the GCS bucket — which a check on 27 October found had not moved since 29 July — carries neither. Rebuilding `geot_projects.json.gz` from the xlsx found two things the rebuild alone would have shipped quietly.

**The status URLs changed carrier mid-format.** August published `Entity Status Data Source URL` as a stringified Python list (`"['https://a', 'https://b']"`); September publishes one semicolon-delimited string, and 73 of its 317 url-bearing rows hold more than one. The August-only parser read such a cell as a single URL, so the evidence link behind a dissolution or amalgamation became a string no browser can open — the one thing those URLs exist to be. Both formats are now read and a multi-URL cell is split whichever wrapper it arrives in, in `scripts/build_geot_projects.py` **and** in the adapter's own copy, which parses the same column from the entities CSV as the fallback for the day the bucket catches up. The two implementations are separate on purpose (the script must not import the backend) and `test_climatetrace.py` now pins them equal, because nothing else would catch the next format change landing on one carrier only.

**A release date cannot postdate its own file.** GEM dates its CSVs `…_DDMMYY.csv` by OpenCheck's convention and documents no order, and the two readings are indistinguishable while the day is 12 or lower. The live set, `…_071026.csv`, was uploaded to the bucket on 29 July 2026: read as day-month it is 7 October 2026, ten weeks *after* the upload, which only the month-day reading fits. Since Phase 313 that date is the `statementDate` on every GEM-derived statement, so production was dating 20,562 entities' ownership from a day that had not happened when the file was published, and `/deepen` reported `gem_release: 2026-10-07`. `_release_date_from_name` now takes a `not_after` stamp — the bucket's `updated` for a GCS download, the file's mtime for the zip fallback — and refuses a parse that lands later than it, logging the refusal; the caller then dates the release by the stamp, as it already did for an unparseable name. The live release now reads 2026-07-29. The digit order itself is still a guess: confirming it with GEM would retire the convention, and until then the invariant is what keeps it honest.

The rebuild itself: 11,435 entities (was 11,097 — 470 added, 132 gone), 544 entity-status records (was 527: 198 dissolved, 122 amalgamated, 226 joint ventures), 125,787 live projects across the nine trackers. Iron Mine Ownership arrived as `GEM asset ID` against the spec's `GEM Asset ID`, which the Phase 142 case-insensitive header lookup absorbed without a build failure. Government of Indonesia gains three bioenergy projects; PT Pertamina is unchanged. The ATTRIBUTIONS citation moves to the September release — it had stayed on May 2026 through two rebuilds, so it now says explicitly that it tracks the artifact's `meta.release`.

Not done here, and worth its own ticket: September's `Entity Ownership` and `Asset Ownership` sheets carry a `Share Imputed?` column the July CSVs do not. If the next bucket refresh ships it, OpenCheck's mapper would still publish every GEM share as `share.exact`, including the imputed ones.
