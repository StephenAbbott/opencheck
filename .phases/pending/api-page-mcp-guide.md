The MCP card on /api had drifted from the server: it named 12 of the 15 tools (missing `opencheck_knowability` from the pre-flight ticket, `opencheck_person_check` and `opencheck_discrepancy_note`) and said nothing the handshake-instructions ticket now tells agents: the order of calls, how to read `degraded_sources` / `kind` / name matches / licence notices, which calls spend the per-address lookup budget, and what a 429 with `retry_after_s` means. It was also hard to read: one card in the 560px endpoint grid with tool names in a squeezed `dt` column, broken mid-word by `break-all`. Now MCP is its own full-width section (`#mcp`, linked from an "On this page" nav beside `#rest`): a Connect card (endpoint, three numbered steps, ARD and `/.well-known/mcp.json`), a "What a call costs" aside, and "Call the tools in this order", six numbered step cards mirroring `_INSTRUCTIONS`. Each tool is its own row (name and arguments over a one-line description), names wrap only at underscores (`<wbr>`), and a "Lookup budget" chip marks the eight tools that spend it. The data lives in `frontend/src/lib/mcpTools.ts`; `backend/tests/test_api_page_mcp.py` parses it and fails when a `TOOL_NAMES` entry is missing or duplicated, when the budget marks differ from the handshake's "Cost." paragraph, or when the steps leave the handshake's order. `ApiPage.test.tsx` pins every tool once, the six steps in order, the budget chips and the reading rules. The design-system baseline drops ApiPage's arbitrary text sizes from 7 to 4. Checked at 1280px and 390px: no horizontal scroll, no console errors. After deploy, check opencheck.world/api renders the section and that the #mcp link scrolls to it.
