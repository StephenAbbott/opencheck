title: A record the lookup accepted on a shared identifier is its source's network subject, so OpenAleph's sanctions-list records of one company name one subject.
readme: OpenAleph records matched to the subject on its registration number now name one declarationSubject, even when the number carries no country.
tests: backend +4

The BODS quality sweep of 23 Nov 2026 failed `declaration_subject_not_subject` on Bank Saderat PLC: two OpenAleph records of the company named two different subjects. OpenAleph returns four records of Bank Saderat PLC (OFAC SDN twice, the US Consolidated Screening List, and the Companies House PSC collection). The three sanctions-list records file `registrationNumber 01126618` with two countries (`ir`, `gb`), so `ftm_registration_country` leaves the number bare (`REG`, the 26 Oct rule), it joins no referent group, and when the deepen cap happened to pick two of them over the PSC record (which carries `REG-GB` and joins) the merged pass had no OpenAleph member to stamp them to. The lookup had already accepted each of them as the subject on that number (`identifier_corroborated`, the FtM match's own gate). `network_subjects` now takes the pipeline's corroborated hit ids (`_corroborated_hit_ids`) and, only for a source with no member in the subject's referent group, makes the lowest `recordId` among that source's corroborated records its subject. Nothing published changes except `declarationSubject`: the number stays bare and no country is inferred. The mapping rides the existing `declaration_subjects` event, so replays and saved reports fold it the same way. Left alone and worth a decision: `_ftm_jurisdiction` takes the first `country` value, so these records carry jurisdiction Iran for a UK company; `ftm_registration_country` already refuses a two-country list. After deploy, re-run the sweep (or `/lookup?lei=2138008KTNTDICZU8L25&refresh=true`) and check every OpenAleph statement names one `declarationSubject`.
