### Points to keep in mind

- **The sources name different ministries.** OpenSanctions has the Ministry of Finance and Wikidata has the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries. Wikidata also lists the **Ministry of Energy as a *former* holder** with no figure ("every path to the company has ended"). So the stake appears to have moved between ministries at some point, and the sources may not all be up to date. Equinor's own shareholder register would settle which ministry holds it now.
- **Threshold used:** 25% or more. No Norwegian national rule is recorded, so OpenCheck falls back to the EU AML Regulation's threshold.
- **No beneficial owners have been declared.** The result has no declared beneficial owners from a register. All the figures above are calculated from shareholdings the sources filed (one layer, direct holdings only). They are context, not a finding that anyone is a beneficial owner. A state shareholding of this kind would normally be treated as state ownership, not as a natural-person beneficial owner.
- **What was read:** OpenSanctions, Global Energy Monitor / Climate TRACE, and Wikidata. The response reports no failed sources (no `degraded_sources`). The rest of Equinor's shares (about 33%, a widely held listed free float) don't show up here because no other single holder reaches 25%.
